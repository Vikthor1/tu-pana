# Citation and Academic Rigor Audit
## "What Would It Mean to Begin with Trust? Designing AI Writing Support for Multilingual Students"

---

## 1. Overall Assessment

The article is scholarly, well-argued, and appropriately grounded in relevant pedagogical literature. The personal narrative is clear and clearly framed as such. The theoretical frameworks are appropriate and correctly identified with the right scholars. **Four citation-level corrections are required before submission:**

1. **Flower & Hayes** — the article incorrectly characterizes their model as "cognitive and social"; it is specifically a **cognitive** process model. The "social" dimension belongs to a separate scholarly tradition.
2. **Anzaldúa** — the quote is **real but truncated**, losing the feminist and bodily dimensions that are central to Anzaldúa's own argument.
3. **Baker-Bell** — her argument is extended beyond its specific scope. Baker-Bell addresses **Anti-Black Linguistic Racism** specifically; the "colonial hierarchies" framing should be attributed to Canagarajah and/or Anzaldúa.
4. **Sommers** — her specific contribution is about **revision strategies**, not the general claim that "writing is thinking." Attribution needs to match her actual argument.

Two minor corrections are also needed in the Further Reading: the Rose subtitle is missing the word "Educationally," and both Sommers and Flower & Hayes are cited in the body but absent from the list.

## 3. Corrections Made in Revised Article

1. **Flower & Hayes**: Removed "and social." Restored their specific contribution as a cognitive process model. Separated Sommers's contribution to its accurate scope (revision as recursive and generative).

2. **Baker-Bell**: Narrowed the Baker-Bell attribution to her specific argument (Anti-Black Linguistic Racism, Black Language). Attributed the "colonial" dimension of linguistic displacement to Canagarajah and Anzaldúa, where it more accurately belongs.

3. **Anzaldúa**: Restored the full verified quote, including "I will have my serpent's tongue — my woman's voice, my sexual voice, my poet's voice. I will overcome the tradition of silence." The complete passage strengthens the article's argument by showing Anzaldúa reclaiming not only racial/linguistic identity but feminist, bodily, and poetic voice simultaneously.

4. **Freire year**: Harmonized to (Freire, 1970/2000) in-text, with full APA 7 entry in Further Reading noting original work published 1968, first English translation 1970.

5. **Writing centers**: Qualified one generalization — "academic writing spaces" → "many academic writing spaces" — to maintain the structural critique without overclaiming universality.

6. **Rose subtitle**: Corrected to "Educationally Underprepared" in Further Reading.

7. **Further Reading**: Reformatted to APA 7; added Sommers (1980) and Flower & Hayes (1981).

## 5. Claims Still Requiring Author Verification

The following items cannot be fully verified through secondary sources and require the author's direct confirmation before submission:

1. **Personal narrative details** — The Michigan State writing center experiences are presented as personal memory. These are appropriate as narrative and do not require citation, but the author should confirm the details are accurately and responsibly represented given that tutors are implicitly described, even without identification.

2. **Anzaldúa quote source location** — The full quote has been confirmed through multiple secondary sources. Before submission to a peer-reviewed outlet, the author should verify the exact wording against the primary text (*Borderlands/La Frontera*, Aunt Lute Books, 1987, Chapter 5 "How to Tame a Wild Tongue"). The wording appears consistent across sources but direct verification is always preferable for an exact quotation.

3. **Freire first English translation year** — The article now cites (1970/2000). The first English translation by Myra Bergman Ramos was 1970 (Herder & Herder); the 30th anniversary edition (2000) was published by Continuum. The author should confirm which edition they are working from and cite accordingly.

4. **Student testimonials** — The article references "students who are first-generation, immigrant, bilingual, Afro-Latinx, Caribbean, or multilingual in any of a dozen configurations" describing deficit-based encounters. This is drawn from the author's teaching observations and is appropriately hedged ("casual conversations"). No additional citation is needed, but the author may wish to note this as observation-based rather than formally collected data.

5. **Tu Pana feature descriptions** — The article describes "Tu Conocimiento," the "Five Questions" framework, the authorship gate, and the Voice Vault. These are accurate based on the app's current design, but should be confirmed against the app's current state before submission in case features have evolved.
