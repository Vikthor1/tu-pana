01 · The problem

What Tu Pana solves

The question AI has put in front of every writing-intensive course: did the student do the thinking?

Generative AI has put writing faculty in an impossible position. Ban it, and student use moves underground while rule-followers lose a tool their peers quietly use. Allow it, and submitted work becomes unverifiable. Rely on detectors, and you get confidence scores that cannot be defended — with multilingual writers running the greatest risk of being wrongly flagged. None of these answers the question that actually matters: did the student do the thinking and the writing?

The stakes are highest for first-generation and multilingual students. They arrive with knowledge and voice their institutions need, but with the least access to the private tutoring and insider know-how that make academic writing feel navigable — which makes an AI ghost-writer most tempting exactly where it does the most damage. These students need support that builds their writing rather than replacing it, in a form their instructors can actually trust.

02 · Why this matters now

Built for the moment universities are in

Higher education is converging on disclosure, authorship, and AI literacy. Tu Pana is built from those principles — not retrofitted to them.

Disclosure

AI use documented, not suspected

Students finish with a process report showing how AI was used along the way — what was asked, what feedback was accepted or questioned. Disclosure becomes evidence, not an honor pledge.

Authorship

The first draft is the student’s alone

An unassisted first-draft gate means AI coaching begins only after the student has written. Authorship isn’t policed after the fact — it’s structured in from the start.

AI literacy

Critical use is the curriculum

Students practice questioning, accepting, and rejecting AI feedback — with their reasoning captured — so they learn to use AI without being used by it.

03 · What makes it different

Not a chatbot, not a detector

Where Tu Pana departs from generic AI assistants, AI detectors, and syllabus-wrapped ChatGPT.

No ghost-writing No detection scores No shortcuts

Coaching with hard boundaries

The coach questions, structures, and gives feedback — and never writes the student’s text, never fabricates sources or data, and never grades. These are enforced design boundaries, not polite suggestions to the model.

Process evidence, not detection

Instead of guessing after submission whether AI wrote a paper, Tu Pana records the writing process itself — drafting, revision decisions, AI interactions — as evidence the student submits with the work.

Bilingual and voice-centered

English/Spanish support runs through the whole journey, and the coach is designed to strengthen how the student already sounds — not flatten them into a generic academic register.

One journey, four disciplines

A single 10-step writing process carries genre-aware pathways for the humanities, community-engaged courses, research writing, and STEM — one coherent tool a program can adopt across the curriculum.

04 · Where this came from

Built from teaching at an HSI

Classroom provenance — who this was designed with, and what the name means.

Tu Pana Writing Studio began in classroom teaching, not in a software lab. It grew out of Dr. Víctor M. Torres-Vélez’s teaching at Eugenio María de Hostos Community College (CUNY) in the South Bronx — a Hispanic-Serving Institution — and it was designed with the students in those classrooms in mind: first-generation, multilingual, Caribbean, Puerto Rican, Latinx, Spanish-speaking, and Central American writers whose voices, languages, and community knowledge are treated as assets to build on, not deficits to correct.

The name carries the design philosophy. In Caribbean and Latin American Spanish, a pana is a trusted friend — the companion who sits beside you and helps you get through. Tu Pana de Escritura is meant to be exactly that: a writing companion, not a ghost-writer — a peer guide that keeps the student doing the writing, supports them across both of their languages, and never replaces their voice with its own.

Research and teaching foundation Scholarship

Tu Pana Writing Studio is informed by Dr. Torres-Vélez’s scholarship and teaching on multilingual pedagogy, open education, writing, and educational equity — much of it written from the same Hostos classrooms the studio grew out of, and translated here into a practical writing environment for the AI era.

05 · Early classroom evidence

From Pilot 1 to Pilot 2

Preliminary design learning from a first classroom pilot — not formal assessment results.

Pilot 2 is not starting from scratch. An initial classroom pilot at Hostos earlier this summer — documented in an internal, anonymized evaluation of student surveys, in-app process reports, submitted essays, and structured tester feedback — showed where the design was resonating with students and where it needed refinement.

The strongest early signal was not simply that students found the tool useful. It was that students experienced the coach as respectful of their voice and language practices — including bilingual and code-switching writing. The same pilot was just as valuable for what it exposed: moments where AI coaching could drift toward supplying language instead of protecting the student’s own, and navigation friction that got in the way of the pedagogy.

Those findings shaped the version now in Pilot 2: stronger authorship gates, clearer process evidence, more careful bilingual support, genre-specific pathways, and a firmer emphasis on AI as coaching rather than replacement writing.

How to read this

These are preliminary classroom observations from a small first pilot, shared to show how the studio learns and improves between pilots. They are not published research, formal assessment results, or proof of outcomes.

08 · What you are comparing

The four genre pathways

One 10-step writing journey — with an unassisted first-draft authorship gate, voice protection, process evidence, and AI-use transparency — carries all four genres.

Student writes firstunassisted first draft → AI coaching beginsonly after the draft exists → Process reportsubmitted with the work
one 10-step journey, every pathway

Autobiographical Mixed-Genre Essay

Ensayo autobiográfico de género mixto

Personal, reflective, analytical writing that connects lived experience, course concepts, and community knowledge. Tu Pana’s base pathway.

Service-Learning Report

Informe de aprendizaje-servicio

Community-engaged writing that connects field experience, observation, reflection, evidence, and public-facing analysis. (Currently profiled for CAP 200 “Bronx Beautiful”; the engine underneath is course-agnostic.)

Research Paper

Trabajo de investigación

Source-based academic writing that develops a research question, evidence, argument, structure, citation, and revision process. The coach never invents, retrieves, or verifies sources or citations.

STEM Lab Report

Informe de laboratorio STEM

Scientific writing (fuller name: STEM Lab Report & Scientific Explanation) that supports lab context, method, observation/data, results, Claim–Evidence–Reasoning, limitations, and AI-use reflection. The coach never fabricates data, solves the science, verifies scientific correctness, or grades.

09 · What to do

Review tasks

About 25–30 minutes total, or review just the pathway closest to your discipline.

  1. Try each pathway for 5–7 minutes. Start where a student starts: write a few honest sentences in the draft panel, walk a few steps of the journey.
  2. Open the support tools. Look at Help, the Five Questions, and “Estoy atascado · I’m stuck.”
  3. Test the coach’s boundaries. Try one thing a shortcut-seeking student would try (e.g., “write it for me”) and watch how the coach responds.
  4. Look at the finish. Open the finish/export flow and skim the process report a student would submit.
  5. Compare across pathways. Genre clarity, support, bilingual access, AI transparency, and finish/export logic.
10 · What to notice

Evaluation checklist

Ten questions — jot quick notes as you go.

  • Is the writing task clear within the first 60 seconds?
  • Does each pathway feel distinct but part of one coherent Tu Pana system?
  • Does the draft area remain central?
  • Do Help, Five Questions, and “I’m stuck” support without overwhelming?
  • Does bilingual support feel empowering rather than heavy?
  • Is AI presented as coaching, not authorship replacement?
  • Does finish/export make sense across all pathways?
  • Does STEM support scientific writing without checking, solving, grading, or fabricating science?
  • What would confuse a real student under deadline pressure?
  • What would help an instructor trust this enough to pilot?

13 · Beyond this review

If this fits your course or program

Tu Pana is growing deliberately — assignment by assignment, pilot by pilot, with process evidence at every step. A first conversation is enough to see whether Tu Pana fits a course, program, or writing-intensive assignment.

For WAC/WID programs and writing-intensive courses across disciplines, the four pathways offer one shared process and one shared evidence standard — adoptable one assignment at a time. Pilots stay small and guarded: your students would receive instructor-issued, assignment-specific links — never the review selector — and you would see the process evidence their work generates.

What a small pilot could look like

One course, one assignment

A single instructor pilots one pathway for one assignment cycle, about 3–4 weeks, using instructor-issued links and process evidence at the end.

A shared assignment across sections

Two or three sections of the same course use the same pathway, helping instructors compare process evidence side by side.

A WAC/WID program look

A program pilots one shared assignment type across disciplines to evaluate fit before anything wider.

Interested? Reach out with your course, the assignment you have in mind, or simply your questions — or include a note with your reviewer feedback.
