# Culturally responsive and disciplinary genre comparison

**Evidence level:** functional local prototype and automated structural checks; student experience
evidence pending

Integrated Desk uses the assignment profile to decide whether cultural, linguistic, family,
community, historical, experiential, or disciplinary knowledge is relevant. Required Path A is
the canonical mixed-genre autobiographical essay; it is not General Writing and is not a fallback.
Neither path requires identity, trauma, family, community, migration, or cultural disclosure.

| Dimension | Path A — mixed-genre autobiographical essay | Path B — STEM lab report |
|---|---|---|
| Relevant knowledge frame | Cultural, linguistic, family, community, historical, and experiential knowledge may support movement from a chosen memory toward analysis. Privacy choice is explicit. | Prior disciplinary knowledge, measurements, observations, methods, uncertainty, and course concepts. No identity narrative is requested. |
| First-use moment | One concise inline **Your knowledge and language may belong here** surface; engage or **Not now**; no modal; revisitable. | No cultural onboarding appears. |
| Genre Moves | Choose a memory and boundary; connect it to a larger force; test experience with research/context; protect language and voice. Each has a concise nudge, why it may help, and an optional persistent note. | Question and prediction; separate observation from interpretation; link evidence to claim. Notes support data, variables, reasoning, limitations, and sources of error. |
| Disclosure protection | Explicitly says identity, trauma, family, migration, and cultural disclosure are optional. A student may choose another entry point and write directly in the Draft. | No autobiographical, admissions, family, community, or cultural prompt leakage. |
| Translingual voice | Onboarding permits English, Spanish, or code-meshing. A Move and exact-text **Protect phrase** action preserve multilingual, family, community, dialectal, and culturally situated language from generic flattening. | Language clarity is disciplinary; the prototype does not demand cultural performance. Multilingual interface choice remains available and student prose remains byte-exact. |
| Focused review | Personal-to-social connection; evidence and historical grounding; voice and translingual integrity. | Claim–evidence alignment; methods and reproducibility; limitations and precision. |
| Council | Connection/structure, evidence/historical-context, and voice/cultural-integrity reviewers; exact roles disclosed before four represented mock calls. | Profile unavailable is stated plainly; zero Council calls are represented. The prototype points to focused review or instructor disciplinary feedback and invents no roles. |
| Contextual critical prompt | Canonical Voice question follows passage coaching; canonical Cultural Knowledge question follows Council. Context cues ask whether mock feedback could genericize, flatten, stereotype, depoliticize, or misread situated writing. Remaining Five Questions stay optional. | Accuracy, specificity, or thinking questions only. The community question is omitted from the contextual framework for this path. |
| Reflection knowledge prompt | Optional cultural, linguistic, family, community, historical, or experiential knowledge prompt, explicitly conditional on the student's choice. | Optional disciplinary knowledge, data, or observations prompt. |
| Finish | Student-controlled checks cover the personal-to-larger-force bridge, traceable factual support, intended voice/phrases, and chosen disclosure boundary. Marks do not gate or rewrite the packet. | General truthful readiness and exact-Draft confirmation; no autobiographical checklist. |
| Evidence | May state that the optional lens was opened/skipped, Move notes exist, exact phrases were protected, or decisions/checks were recorded. It never claims disclosure, cultural understanding, or voice quality. | May state that disciplinary notes, reviews, decisions, or versions exist. It never claims methodological mastery. |

General Writing remains an explicitly selected neutral profile with purpose, audience, evidence, and
structure guidance. Admissions and SOP retain their own configured Moves. An unknown assignment id
renders a configuration-required stop and inherits neither autobiography nor General Writing.

## Functional contrast result

Automation verified that autobiographical onboarding is inline, optional, revisitable, translingual,
and nonblocking; exact Move notes and protected phrases persist without changing the Draft; coach and
Council disclosures identify exact payloads; and the student controls decision rationale and Finish
checks. Switching the same isolated state to STEM removed the onboarding and autobiographical
language, presented disciplinary Moves and reflection, and exposed the unavailable Council profile
explicitly. Targeted checks also found zero autobiographical Move/onboarding leakage in admissions,
SOP, or General Writing, and a loud stop for an unknown assignment. The canonical Draft remained
byte-exact across language changes and reload.

This proves profile routing and leakage prevention in the prototype. It does not prove that the
autobiographical path feels welcoming rather than imposed, that translingual students feel protected, or
that STEM students recognize the disciplinary guidance as appropriate. Those are lived-evidence
questions.
