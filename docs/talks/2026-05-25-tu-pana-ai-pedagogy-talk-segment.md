# Tu Pana de Escritura — AI That Doesn't Write for Students

*Faculty talk segment — approximately 4 minutes at a measured pace (~590 words spoken).*
*Drafted 2026-05-25. Adapt freely for your specific audience and context.*

---

I want to start with the concern I hear most often from colleagues, and I want to take it seriously: *What if the AI just does it for them?*

It's the right question. Most AI writing tools, if we're honest, are optimized for output. They are designed to produce text quickly, fluently, and on demand. And that design — however useful it may be in other contexts — is a problem in a writing classroom, because it bypasses the very process that makes writing educationally meaningful. When a student hands you an essay that an AI drafted, you are not reading evidence of that student's thinking. You are reading a simulation of thinking. The labor — the struggle to locate oneself in relation to an idea, to find the argument, to claim a voice — has been outsourced.

Tu Pana is built on the premise that this labor is not a problem to be solved. It is the point.

The app functions as what I would call a constrained Socratic coach. It asks questions. It reflects ideas back. It challenges assumptions and offers structured frameworks for revision. What it does not do — and is designed at the architectural level to prevent — is generate text for the student. This is not a technical limitation. It is a foundational pedagogical and ethical choice. The constraint exists because we believe that the student's intellectual labor cannot be transferred to an AI without losing what the writing is actually for.

The structure of the app enforces this. Students move through ten writing stages — drafting, researching, revising, reflecting — and at Stage 6, before any AI-assisted revision is available, there is an authorship gate. Students must write and save an unassisted first draft. That draft is documented. It is a fixed point in the process. It is what allows us to say, with evidence, that the thinking in this essay originated with this student. At the end of the process, the app generates an Instructor Process Report — a transparent record of session engagement, revision decisions, and self-assessment — precisely so that you, as the instructor, do not have to take authenticity on faith.

Now, I want to say something about our students specifically, because the generic version of this argument stops here, and I think that's a mistake.

Many of our students at Hostos are multilingual. They write in Spanish, in English, across both. They bring into their essays forms of knowledge — family history, migration experience, community memory — that do not always map neatly onto standardized academic prose. What most AI tools do with that language is correct it. They treat bilingual expression as error. They apply pressure toward a monolingual academic norm that was not designed with our students in mind.

Tu Pana takes the opposite position. All student-facing text exists in both Spanish and English simultaneously. Code-switching and bilingual expression are treated as legitimate rhetorical resources — not errors. And at Stage 8, students have access to what we call the Voice Vault: a feature that lets them mark specific phrases as protected, rendering their own language explicitly off-limits for AI modification. The student decides what gets kept. The student decides what belongs.

During revision, students use what we call the Five Questions — a fixed protocol focused on Accuracy, Voice, Specificity, Thinking, and Cultural Knowledge — to evaluate the coach's feedback and log their own revision decisions. They are not passive recipients of the AI's assessment. They are the authority over their own text.

[*Optional close for skeptical audiences:*]
I know some of you are still wondering whether this can really hold in practice. My answer is: the architecture holds because we designed it to hold. The authorship gate is not optional. The Voice Vault is not advisory. The coaching is constrained at the system level, not just at the prompt level. This is what it looks like to build AI around student dignity rather than around productivity. And I think that distinction is worth defending — especially here, especially with these students.

## Adaptation notes

- **For skeptical senior faculty:** Lead with the authorship gate and Instructor Process Report — the accountability structure tends to land first with this audience.
- **For writing center / composition audiences:** Expand the Five Questions section; they will recognize the revision-authority framework immediately.
- **For DEI or equity-focused events:** Move the bilingual parity and Voice Vault paragraphs earlier — that framing should precede the technical description.
- **To shorten to 3 minutes:** Cut the final bracketed paragraph and trim the bilingual section to two sentences (~100 words removed).
