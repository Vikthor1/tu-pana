# Tu Pana de Escritura: Teaching Writing in the Age of AI
### A Script for Faculty Audiences

---

**[OPENING — 90 seconds]**

I want to start with a question I hear constantly from colleagues.

*"My students are using AI to write their papers. What do I do?"*

And I understand the panic behind that question. Because what we are really asking is: *Has writing instruction become obsolete?*

I want to argue the opposite.

I want to argue that this moment — right now, when AI can produce a passable five-paragraph essay in eleven seconds — is actually the most important moment in the history of writing pedagogy. Because for the first time, we are being forced to ask what writing is *actually for*.

And when you ask that question honestly, you realize that AI has not replaced writing. It has just exposed everything we were doing wrong.

---

**[THE PROBLEM — 2 minutes]**

Let me describe a student I know. I'll call her Mariela.

Mariela is a first-generation college student. Her family speaks Spanish at home. She works thirty hours a week. She sits down to write her first college essay and she faces something that none of our rubrics or handouts was designed to address.

She is not struggling because she lacks intelligence. She is struggling because she is being asked to write in a voice that is not hers, in an academic register she has never been taught, about experiences that most of her professors have no framework for, using a process that was designed for students whose parents went to college.

And here is what happens. She freezes. Or she writes something generic and safe. Or — increasingly — she opens ChatGPT and pastes in the prompt.

And we blame her. Or we blame AI. But I want to suggest that we have been asking the wrong question.

The question is not *how do we stop students from using AI?*

The question is *why does the blank page feel so threatening that AI feels like the only way through it?*

---

**[THE INSIGHT — 2 minutes]**

Here is what I learned after years of teaching writing at a community college with a student body that is over 90% Latino, majority first-generation, majority multilingual.

The blank page is not an invitation. For students like Mariela, it is an accusation.

It says: *Prove you belong here. In this language. In this register. Right now.*

Most writing instruction responds to this by teaching grammar, or thesis statements, or citation formats. These are real skills. But they are skills that live at the surface of a problem that lives much deeper.

The deeper problem is this: Mariela does not know that her story is material. She does not know that the way her grandmother described the flight from Puerto Rico is a form of knowledge. She does not know that her code-switching, her mixed syntax, her bilingual mind — these are assets, not deficits.

Before she can write a college essay, she needs someone to tell her that she is already a writer.

That is what Tu Pana de Escritura is designed to do.

---

**[WHAT IT IS — 2 minutes]**

*Tu Pana* means *your buddy* in Caribbean Spanish slang. Tu Pana de Escritura is your writing buddy.

It is a web application — free, open, runs in any browser, no login required — that guides students through a ten-stage scaffolded writing process designed specifically for first-generation, multilingual, and community college writers.

But I want to be precise about what *scaffolded* means here, because I think that word gets overused.

Most scaffolding in writing instruction looks like this: here is a template, fill in the boxes. That is not scaffolding. That is a form.

Real scaffolding looks like this: before you write a single sentence, we are going to do something together. We are going to find the anecdote — the specific, sensory, lived moment — that is the seed of your argument. We are going to protect it. We are going to build around it.

Tu Pana starts at Stage 1 with what I call the *anecdote lab*. The student is not asked to write an essay. They are asked: *Tell me about a moment. One moment. Something you saw, heard, felt, smelled. Something that stayed with you.*

That is the entry point. Not the thesis. Not the argument. The moment.

And from that moment, the entire essay grows.

---

**[THE INNOVATION — 3 minutes]**

Now here is where I want to talk about what makes this genuinely different from other writing tools — including AI writing tools.

There is a concept built into Tu Pana called the **Voice Vault**.

The Voice Vault is where students deposit the most authentic language they produce during the early stages — the sentences that sound like *them*, the words they would use at home, the phrases that come naturally before academic pressure normalizes everything.

Why does this matter?

Because here is what happens to first-generation multilingual students in traditional writing instruction. They start with an authentic voice. The feedback they receive — well-intentioned feedback — tells them to be more formal, more academic, more standard. By the fifth draft, the essay sounds like nobody. It has been corrected into anonymity.

Tu Pana does the opposite. It protects the early voice and uses it as the standard against which revisions are measured. The question is not *is this more academic?* The question is *does this still sound like you?*

This is pedagogically important. But it is also *politically* important.

Because when you tell a Latina student at a community college that her language is an asset to protect, not a deficit to correct — you are making an argument about whose knowledge counts in a college classroom.

Now, about AI.

Tu Pana does include an AI writing coach. But the way it uses AI is deliberately different from tools like ChatGPT or essay generators.

The AI coach does not write for the student. It does not suggest sentences. It reads what the student has written and returns a *diagnosis* — specific issues, named and explained. Then, the vast majority of follow-up guidance happens *locally*, without another AI call. The student gets a menu of specific next steps based on the diagnosis, and they choose.

This means the student is always the agent. The AI is a reader, not a ghost-writer.

And critically: the process is designed so that *skipping it does not help you*. If you paste in an AI-generated paragraph, the AI coach will read it and flag exactly what it lacks — specificity, voice, grounding in the anecdote you built in Stage 1. The scaffolding creates accountability that generic AI output cannot satisfy.

---

**[FOR FACULTY — 2 minutes]**

I want to speak directly to the faculty in this room for a moment.

I know what some of you are thinking. *This sounds like a lot of technology for a writing class. My students need to learn to sit with difficulty. They need to develop patience and stamina, not more tools.*

I agree with you completely.

Tu Pana is not designed to reduce difficulty. It is designed to redirect it.

The difficulty of staring at a blank page and not knowing where to start is not productive difficulty. It is paralysis. It teaches students nothing except that they do not belong.

The difficulty of sitting with a specific memory and finding the language to make someone else feel what you felt — that is the real work of writing. Tu Pana creates the conditions for that difficulty to be productive.

For faculty, this tool is also a teaching object. You can use it to show students what a writing process looks like before they internalize one of their own. You can assign specific stages. You can use the Voice Vault concept as a framework for discussing revision. You can use the anecdote lab as a stand-alone exercise in any class.

You do not need to use the whole application. The ideas embedded in it are as useful as the software.

---

**[THE BIGGER PICTURE — 1.5 minutes]**

Let me zoom out for a moment.

We are living through a crisis of writing in higher education. AI has made it easy to fake the product of writing. And our response — plagiarism detectors, honor codes, blue book exams — is a defensive response. It is a response that assumes the game is over.

I want to argue for an offensive response.

The offensive response is to teach writing in a way that AI cannot replicate. Not because we lock students away from technology, but because we teach them to want something that AI cannot give them.

What AI cannot give you is the feeling of finding your own sentence. The moment when the right word arrives and you know it is right because it is yours. The experience of writing something that surprises you.

That experience — and the confidence it produces — is what our students are being denied when they default to AI. Not because they are lazy or dishonest. But because no one ever showed them that they were capable of it.

Tu Pana de Escritura is built on the belief that they are.

---

**[CLOSE — 1 minute]**

I will end where I began.

*"My students are using AI to write their papers. What do I do?"*

Here is my answer.

Build a process so specific to their lives, so grounded in their actual experience, so protective of their actual voice, that AI-generated text is obviously inadequate by comparison.

Not because it is lower quality. But because it is not *theirs*.

That is what writing is for. That is what education is for.

Tu Pana de Escritura is my attempt to build that process for the students who need it most.

Thank you.
